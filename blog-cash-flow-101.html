Cash Flow 101: Reading Your Numbers Without the Jargon

Plenty of profitable businesses run into serious trouble — not because they're not making money, but because they run out of cash. Here's how to tell the difference.

Profit and cash are not the same thing

Profit is what's left after expenses are subtracted from revenue on paper. Cash flow is what's actually moving in and out of your bank account. A business can be profitable and still run out of cash — usually because money owed to it (unpaid invoices) is sitting out there while bills, wages, and suppliers still need to be paid now.

The three things a simple cash flow report tells you

  • What's coming in. Cash actually received — not invoiced, received. Late-paying clients can make a business look far healthier on paper than it feels in the bank account.
  • What's going out. Every regular and one-off payment — wages, suppliers, rent, subscriptions, loan repayments.
  • What's left, and when. The gap between the two, tracked over time, shows you whether you're building a cushion or slowly running one down.

Warning signs worth watching for

A few patterns tend to show up before a cash flow problem becomes a crisis: outstanding invoices creeping further past their due dates, relying on a single large client for most of your income, or covering short-term gaps with a credit card rather than planned cash reserves. None of these are catastrophic on their own — but they're worth noticing early, while there's still time to act.

Where Ledgerly fits in: monthly cash flow reporting is part of our standard bookkeeping service — plain-English reports that show what's actually happening in your bank account, not just what's technically profitable on paper, so you can catch a tightening cash position months before it becomes a problem.

A simple habit that helps most

Reviewing a basic cash flow summary monthly — even just ten minutes with your bookkeeper or your own reports — is usually enough to catch a slow squeeze early. The businesses that get caught out are almost always the ones that only look at their bank balance, rather than the trend behind it.
